Understand the cost-access labels

Community health centers/FQHCs: Insurance is not required. Fees are adjusted using income and family size. Patients at or below 100% of the federal poverty guideline receive a full discount or may pay a nominal charge, depending on the center’s policy and service.

NJ Charity Care: This is hospital financial assistance for eligible uninsured or underinsured patients. An application and screening are required, and some separate professional, prescription, or other charges may not be covered.

Published flat fee: NearSignal shows one only when a current authoritative source confirms the exact service and price.

How NearSignal orders these options

Safety gates come first: emergency selections show only verified hospital emergency departments appropriate for the selected adult or child population. Age and published capability limits are applied next. Eligible options are then ordered by care-setting fit, access needs, and, when available, current driving duration. Unsupported wait, insurance, and cost claims are excluded.

What “verified” means

Facility identity, address, care setting, adult/child population, phone, displayed hours, listed capabilities, and access claims are tied to provider or government sources. Information can change—call before non-emergency travel.

About this pilot

Built carefully from the start.

NearSignal is currently a public pilot covering a curated set of verified adult and pediatric emergency, urgent-care, and community health-center options. It is not exhaustive, does not assess symptoms, and does not know capacity, wait time, final cost, or insurance network status. Access labels describe verified programs, not individual eligibility. Facility and government pages remain the source of truth.

First: protect the clock and the paper trail

Ask the provider to place the account on an administrative hold while it is disputed. Request the itemized bill, Explanation of Benefits, claim and denial codes, network records, any estimate, and every notice or consent form. Keep screenshots, names, dates, and confirmation numbers.

  1. Open an insurer appealUse the appeal address or portal in the denial notice. State that network information was relied on, identify the disputed charges, request reprocessing at the in-network level, and ask for all records used to decide the claim.
  2. Preserve the deadlineUse the deadline printed on the denial or plan documents. Many employer-plan claim appeals allow at least 180 days, but your notice and plan control. File something in writing before the deadline even if more evidence will follow.
  3. Escalate outside the insurerFederal and state complaint channels can review whether surprise-billing or insurance rules were followed. Employer plans may also fall under the U.S. Department of Labor.

Plain-language appeal starter

Subject: Urgent request to review and reprocess disputed medical claim

I am appealing the handling of this claim and asking that collection activity be paused while it is reviewed. Before care, I relied on information indicating that the facility or service was in network. I did not knowingly choose an out-of-network service at this price.

Please provide the complete claim file, the specific plan terms and codes used, all network-status records, and any notice or consent form you believe applies. Please review applicable federal and state surprise-billing protections and reprocess covered charges at the in-network cost-sharing level when required. Please confirm receipt and all appeal deadlines in writing.

Do not send medical records, member IDs, or other personal information through NearSignal. Use the insurer or regulator’s secure channel. For legal advice or a lawsuit, contact a qualified attorney.
